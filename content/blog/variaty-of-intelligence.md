The need to survive and reproduce are common to all living things. Then why were humans able to achieve a different level of intelligence? Can we say that the differences in intelligence and intelligence levels between animals are purely due to their environment? So why do some races develop intelligence so quickly, and who doesn't? If differences in intelligence threaten to cause extinction, why do some of them fail and become extinct? There are so many ways to thrive besides advancing intelligence (eg. Symbiosis, parasitism) Why did humanity develop something called intelligence? Is it the most intelligent strategy among many other racial development methods? If intelligence has grown to help us survive, why do we sometimes commit suicide in agony? Is our intelligence an imperfect survival strategy in terms of causing suicide, or is intelligence irrelevant to survival strategies in the first place? Or are the moments when we choose to commit suicide caused not by intelligence but by other imperfections (e.g., the hypertrophy of self-selection)? The method of explaining the higher dimension from natural selection is so interdependent that each of the connecting steps is difficult to prove, and there are too many of them. However, even if it is successfully discovered, the explanation direction is not the only one. Just because it was the only one, it could not be concluded that the actual causal process was the one that happened. In other words, the principle of natural selection is a logical development method that leaves many loopholes that can be rejected. The world is often the result of multiple people. Explaining one cause does not disqualify all other causes from being false. We should not try to climb this elusive narrow ladder, but rather find it more intuitive or more robust.
